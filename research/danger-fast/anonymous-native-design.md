# Generic anonymous-card C# research prototype

Status: isolated opt-in source, 2026-10-30. No production route, worker contract, release or browser-latency claim. Lead owns compilation, measurements and local commits. Agent has not committed separately.

## Source and scope

`PostDrawDangerAnonymous.cs` extends the existing private `OrderedResolver` through a partial class, compiled only through `Research.targets`. `DangerAnonymousExperiment.cs` consumes the frozen inputs and journals Started/Failed/Sample records before assertions, with source/assembly/input hashes. Default experimental cap is 16,384 frontier entries and 140M Work. Work includes a reserved result-finalization estimate; UI/wire/serialization is outside this harness.

Guarded scope: active byte-packable decks, no hidden deck-loss operations, bounded search plans, pre-draw costs or minimum-hand-size activation gates. Ordinary simple optional terminal searches are supported. These exclusions are explicit applicability limits, not silently changed model semantics. Draw locks, fixed FIFO/custom activation priority, per-entry allowances and postdraw policy removals use existing helpers. The harness reconstructs frozen active cases; it is not evidence for disabled-input compatibility.

## State invariant

Pool only cards with no draw effect and no search whose canonical identity differs from every possible Danger source. Keep all effect/search entries and ordinary same-Danger-identity cards explicit. The existing state vector's pooled deck slots contain the **unobserved ordinary inventory**, its hand slots contain known held counts, and two appended packed scalars are anonymous held count U and physical ordinary deck count P. Inventory minus U minus P is anonymous random loss. Nonpooled deck slots remain actual deck counts; FIFO, OPT, activation and completion fields retain their original meanings.

Conditional on the observable history, inventory labels are uniform over U held, P deck and remaining lost ordinary slots. An ordinary draw changes `(U,P)` to `(U+1,P-1)` without observing a label. An anonymous random discard changes U to U-1 without changing inventory or P. Revealing U labels uses the multivariate hypergeometric distribution, subtracts revealed counts from inventory and adds them to known hand. A later known discard removes a known card without changing inventory. These operations preserve the exchangeability invariant; the complete observation proof is developed in `aggregate.md`.

All stored masses are normalized integer numerators over `D=N!*S`, with S the existing random-discard scale. Draw and random-discard transitions multiply by their physical multiplicity and divide by physical deck/hand size. Every division is checked with `BigInteger.DivRem`. Hypergeometric conditioning also asserts integral mass. No floating arithmetic determines endpoints, state selection or certification.

## Policy observations and reuse

Before an identity-observing postdraw removal, reveal all U held identities, then run actual `CompleteBatch`, `SelectCandidate` and `Activate`. Scope excludes pre-cost/hand-minimum observations; no caller is allowed to hide U from such a test. Physical random discards copy the existing queue-aware branch logic, including adjacent-run multiplicity and removal of the actual source token. An anonymous discard is always different-identity by the partition precondition.

One generic no-reveal exception is implemented: exactly one postdraw discard; every eligible pooled row is positive-requirement relevant; every eligible explicit row has no search; and at least one eligible held explicit row is not relevant. Unknown eligible cards are tier1, while that explicit candidate is tier2/3/4. All relevant policy tests are independent of U under the scope guard, so the actual `RemoveCard` chooses the same explicit card without revelation. This is a tier-domination proof, not a check for an Allure name, ID or fixture layout. A removable search source invalidates the exception because its keep tier could observe hidden held targets.

## Terminal conditioning and cache

The terminal cache stores conditional win vectors independent of incoming mass. Its denominator is `I!`, where I is remaining unobserved inventory size. Reveal U under that unit; if necessary allocate the remaining physical deck P jointly from the unrevealed inventory, with hidden losses its complement. Evaluate each concrete hand/deck through existing `PhysicalSearchHandCounter`, obtaining all combo masks and their actual group/total unions.

After U is revealed, each ordinary deck capacity lies between `max(0,inventory_i-hiddenLoss)` and `min(inventory_i,P)`. Optional-search success is monotone in available deck capacities for a **fixed known hand**, including combo maxima/exclusions: every old witness remains available when capacities grow. If both extremes yield the same Boolean for every combo, every actual deck allocation has that mask and the whole conditional mass can be classified without enumeration. Otherwise the complete joint allocation is enumerated; no independence assumption is made.

Cache keys retain pooled inventories, all known held counts, U/P, and explicit searchable deck capacities capped by the maximum searches available from held sources. Non-target explicit deck counts and terminal policy/FIFO fields are unobservable at this boundary and omitted. Capping uses the same optional-search capacity argument as the existing physical terminal cache. Conditional fractions are multiplied by incoming mass with exact divisibility checks before committing the entire terminal atomically.

## Bounds, interruption and accounting

Every classified terminal or mandatory failed-removal branch adds its full mass to R; each row's success adds to S_r. All unresolved, evicted and interrupted outcomes remain in `D-R`, so `[S_r/D,(S_r+D-R)/D]` encloses every row. Terminal computation finishes and every throwing budget call completes before changing S or R. An exception stops the entire traversal; no partly expanded parent is retried. A partially constructed conditional cache value is never published.

Frontiers store packed vectors and integer mass. The bounded priority index references those same entries and charges its reference payload separately; aggregate retained-cell telemetry includes current/next frontiers, priority references, conditional cache, binomial table and active resolution-stack estimate. Comparisons charge Work inside the ordered-set comparator. A Work exception during index mutation ends the entire calculation; the index is never reused. By-value dictionary updates preserve the existing AOT GC safety convention.

Remaining review points: allocation/object overhead and aggregate telemetry are estimates, not a total-memory ceiling; initial/harness transport work remains outside production acceptance; independent small labeled-card tests and adversarial observation cases are still required. Current source has not yet been compiled or measured by its author; the lead handles serial checks. Do not claim the JS fixture-scoped result as native C# or browser evidence.

## First native evidence and priority revision

The lead compiled and measured the initial sorted-set implementation at 140M Work. Heavy40 returned width 28.19161197pp after 19,033 expansions (1,405.26ms native); heavy60 returned 15.25159478pp after 12,583 expansions (940.62ms). All six frozen references were enclosed, but these results are **too wide**. Conditional terminal caching was effective (709 evaluated terminal splits on each case; 22,432 / 16,790 cache hits), shifting attention to priority work and normalized mass arithmetic. These exact source/assembly/input hashes and full endpoints are journaled in `anonymous-results.jsonl`, and the lead preserved source/evidence in local commit `d7652ba` before editing.

The next prototype replaces comparison-tree priority with finite bins indexed by mass bit length and its four leading bits. All weights in one bin have similar magnitude; ties use insertion FIFO. Approximate ranking affects only which disjoint mass to refine or evict, never interval arithmetic. The next frontier evicts from its smallest bin; the current frontier is processed from largest bins first, releasing each entry immediately. This addresses two separate costs: repeated large-integer comparisons and spending most Work on low-mass insertion-order entries while large mass remains pending.

Bucket descriptors and linked-node references have an explicit independent Storage charge (2 cells per descriptor plus 11 per retained entry), together with the packed-key/integer map's own limit and aggregate telemetry. `PriorityWork` and `ScaleWork` are disjoint subsets of total charged Work, enabling bottleneck assessment without double-counting. The current bucket source has not yet been measured at the time of this note; lead runs follow. More efficient priority representation must not be described as achieved accuracy until the new samples complete.

The first independent native oracle run passed 22 containment/convergence cases. The initial forced-interruption test incorrectly assumed the final Work charge preceded the last terminal commit; a legal Work stop can occur while scanning empty branches after all mass is resolved. The test now binary-searches the earliest budget reaching R=D and checks the immediately preceding budget. This corrects the test assumption, not the interval algorithm. Its rerun is pending at this checkpoint.

## Bucket evidence and whole-batch quotient

The corrected 23-case native oracle run passed. At 140M Work, buckets improved heavy40/60 widths to 16.7063pp / 8.04304pp, still too wide. The lead's **diagnostic-only larger Work** runs (unchanged Standard Storage) reached 9.49pp / 4.077pp at 350M, and 2.36738pp / 0.66328pp at 700M. The latter still stopped on Work after 90,831 / 76,347 expansions. These are not Fast-policy compliance results. Priority consumed about 39M / 43M and scaling about 156M / 216M of the 700M budget, so priority alone cannot close the remaining cost gap. Local checkpoint `bb201d2` preserves the bucket baseline.

`PostDrawDangerAnonymousBatch.cs` now implements an optional exact batch quotient; default remains off. Enable with `YGO_DANGER_BATCHES=1`; the journal includes the flag and both algorithm source hashes. For d pending draws from R physical cards, enumerate counts k_i for explicit rows and u for the anonymous physical pool P. Let E be the total newly eligible draw-effect tokens (eligibility is fixed until the batch finishes). For each distinct FIFO word of those effect tokens, the integer numerator is

`(d!/E!) * C(P,u) * product_i C(deck_i,k_i) * product_candidate_i k_i!`,

and the denominator is the falling factorial `(R)_d`. The selected effect copies are physically labeled; their within-row label permutations supply k_i!, while d!/E! sums all unobservable non-effect positions/orders. The sum across count vectors and distinct effect words is one. Existing FIFO tokens remain first; new tokens append in their enumerated order. Policy completion, removals and subsequent activations occur only after all d draws, exactly as before. Pooled inventory remains unrevealed; U increases and P decreases by u. Search order is excluded by the existing scope guard.

The new frontier may mix different visible depths because one transition can consume different batch sizes. No probability calculation depends on layer depth: each full state carries remaining physical capacity and normalized mass, so combining identical states and keeping other states separate remains valid. The opt-in oracle fixture now covers all ten anonymous shapes with both one-draw and whole-batch modes, including fixed-budget repeatability, enclosure, exact convergence and forced terminal-commit interruption. New runs are pending at this checkpoint; source alone establishes no performance claim.

The lead subsequently ran all 34 opt-in oracle cases successfully. Whole-batch heavy measurements at 140M Work were **worse**: approximately 36.31pp / 19.17pp width, after 784 / 656 expanded batches. ## Further hypothesis if batch exploration remains too costly

Unimplemented: use lower subprobability prefix masses on a fixed grid such as `D=2^60`. Every transition rounds its nonnegative mass downward using exact integer multiplication/division; all lost mass remains unresolved. This can reduce mass payload and use UInt128 for guarded small transition factors. At a terminal with conditional success q, accumulate `floor(m*q)` as success and `floor(m*(1-q))` as failure separately for each row. Return `[success/D, (D-failure)/D]`. **Do not reuse the common resolved-mass formula unchanged**: separately rounded terminal success and failure need not sum to m. Exact terminal conditional caches can be reused, and zero/one masks retain exact grid mass. A full proof must bound the generated conditional partitions, account for every rounding loss, preserve rowwise unions and charge conversion/finalization. Small bounded denominators are not permission to use unproved floating arithmetic or move lost mass into success.

A separate unimplemented quotient could retain a second anonymous stratum after each policy revelation/removal. Keep the known ordinary hand's multiset as inventory K and its retained-slot count V. Subsequent random Danger discards choose this stratum with multiplicity V and decrement V instead of branching over each known ordinary category. Newly drawn ordinary cards continue to use the existing unobserved inventory I, anonymous held U and deck P. At the next policy observer or terminal, reveal V from K and U from I with separate hypergeometric partitions. After the deterministic operation, the resulting known ordinary hand can become a fresh K stratum. **Never merge K with I**: the original known-hand labels have a different conditional location distribution from deck/loss labels. A proof must establish the two within-stratum permutation symmetries conditional on all stratum-level selections, and preserve same-Danger-identity exclusion, costs/hand observers and terminal search conditioning. This targets known-card discard branching after Graceful; it is not yet evidence of narrower or cheaper native bounds.

## Two-stratum native variant (subsequent implementation)

The second hypothesis above is now implemented behind `retainedStratum=false` / `YGO_DANGER_RETAINED_STRATUM=1`. Baseline checkpoint `52ca458` preserves the previous source. Combining it with whole-batch mode is explicitly rejected for the first experiment. Pooled hand slots hold K, an extra packed scalar holds V, and all random-discard physical hand sizes use V rather than the inventory sum. The category-row discard loop skips pooled rows and adds one V-weighted branch. Previously observed lost labels remain outside I and the physical deck.

`RevealBoth` samples a V-subset from K and then reveals U from I. Before actual policy removal, its callback supplies a fully observed known hand with V equal to its pooled-card count. After removal, the remaining multiset becomes the fresh K and V is reset to its size. The skip-revelation tier-dominance case keeps the old K/V unchanged because its selected explicit row cannot belong to either ordinary stratum. Terminal cache keys retain K, V, I, U and P; their conditional unit is now `K! * I!`, not merely I!.

The native oracle harness adds all ten prior shapes for this variant plus a seven-physical-card fixture with effect-first priority: two Danger sources, draw-two/filter-A-discard-one, two A cards, B and overlap. This leaves later random-discard/draw opportunities after an ordinary observation. Its complete run must record both retained-stratum discards and nontrivial `0<V<K` revelations, so passing simple cases cannot accidentally leave the new logic untested. Final-commit interruption is checked with the flag as well. Source is saved; independent review and the lead's build/tests/measurements are pending at this checkpoint. This remains isolated research, not enabled production support.

The lead's first two-stratum run passed all 47 then-current oracle cases. At 140M Work and capacity 2,048, heavy40/60 widths were 7.249916pp / 2.387841pp. All frozen reference rows were enclosed. This is a modest improvement, **still missing the initial 2pp width goal**, and does not justify production enablement. Independent review (`two-stratum-review.md`) found no mathematical blocker under the scope gate.

Coverage was then strengthened without changing probability transitions: `JointStratumReveals` records simultaneous uncertain K and nonempty U; `EmptyRetainedReveals` records K labels that must stay excluded from the deck after V reaches zero; `RetainedObserverReveals` records a subsequent policy observation of an uncertain retained stratum. The seven-card case asserts joint and V=0 paths. A second seven-card case replaces one A copy with a later-priority draw-one/discard-one source, forcing another policy observation after the random loss. The 48-case rerun is pending at this note's checkpoint. The lead is measuring the frozen challenge set and a near-Fast-limit case before restoring a normal build and making the final local research checkpoint. No AOT/browser latency or public-result integration has been established.

The first expanded run passed 47/48 tests; the repeated-observer test incorrectly also required a joint K/U event that its deck did not produce. Joint and V=0 requirements remain on the original two-stratum fixture, where both passed. The repeated-observer fixture now targets its own obligation and filters the second discard to A, preventing a newly drawn nonrelevant Danger from legitimately shielding the uncertain K pool through tier dominance. A concrete trace is opener special+Danger+A; draw B+overlap, discard overlap; K={A,B}; randomly discard one retained ordinary card, draw the second observer; draw the last Danger; the filtered-A removal must observe which K card survived. The corrected focused rerun is pending. This is a test-design correction, not a probability transition change.

A small future normalization is available for investigation: once V=0, K contains only permanently lost labels. Clearing K while leaving I/P unchanged should preserve all future observations and could merge otherwise equivalent states. Its observation proof is straightforward under the present no-return-to-deck scope, but it has not been implemented or measured; current tests deliberately retain K to exercise the separation of lost labels from I.
